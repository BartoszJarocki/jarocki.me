how a neural network learns

a model is a function with knobs. this one has two knobs, w and b, and draws the line y = w·x + b through eight points. the loss says how badly the line misses, averaged over all the points. every setting of the two knobs has a loss, so together they form a landscape. the cube is the model. each step, it feels the slope under it and moves downhill. that is gradient descent.

the learning rate is the size of each step. at 0.03 the cube crawls. at 0.38 it overshoots the valley and bounces from wall to wall. past about 0.42 every bounce lands higher than the last, and the model gets worse the longer it trains. click the surface to drop the cube somewhere else, and drag to turn it.

a network sees each point only as two numbers, its position. we show it points of two kinds, filled and hollow, and it has to learn which is which. behind the points, every cell shows the network's answer for that spot. the darker the cell, the surer it is that a point there is filled. the thin line marks where it can't decide.

the squares on the right are the first layer's weights, one row per neuron. a filled square is a positive weight, a hollow one is negative, and the bigger the square, the stronger the weight. point at a row to draw the line that neuron cuts the plane along. the next layer bends those straight lines into curves.

here is the same network, drawn as layers from left to right. each column is one neuron, and its height is how strongly it fires for one input point. a filled column fires positive, a hollow one negative. the lines between layers are the weights. a darker line is a stronger weight, and a dashed line is a negative one. move over the small square to feed the network a different point.

drag to turn the network. the last column is its answer. it rises toward full height when the network thinks the point is filled, and sinks when it thinks the point is hollow.
